# MAIM phased publishing and reader acquisition plan
26 October 2026 · Working production plan reflecting founder direction · No launch date or price approval implied.

## Founder direction and scope
Aim to complete all books and collections. Practical first-wave ambition: three to four finished books in each of four collections, then add further work roughly quarterly as quality and capacity allow. Do not rush drafts to manufacture a large catalogue. This is an ambition of 12–16 items, not a verified inventory or a requirement to delay every ready collection until all others finish. Keep all currently authorized source recovery and editorial work moving.

A finished book must have a complete reader promise and useful outcome on its own. A sample chapter, cover variant, lesson, outline or lightly repackaged duplicate is not another finished book.

### Do not transfer unchanged
- Pricing scenario prescribes three tiers and deliberate decoys; MAIM should offer only genuinely useful choices and an accurate comparison. Three is not a universal law.
- Product-launch scenario recommends inflated assigned bonus values and aggressive anchors. Use actual prices and substantiated comparisons; no imaginary reference values.
- Sunk-cost pressure, manufactured out-groups and polarizing belonging conflict with independent reader choice. Keep community welcoming and cancellation/declining easy.
- The named-story fallback can encourage unsupported first-person details or imply a real client engagement. Require supplied founder testimony/permission, or an explicitly hypothetical example without invented results.
- Its fixed sample thresholds and claims of optimal bias stacks are heuristics, not a statistical power analysis or guaranteed uplift. Low traffic should lead to cautious interpretation and reader interviews, not manufactured significance.
- Keep adult educational promotion directed to relevant buyers. For child/teen Integrity editions, address parents, educators and responsible facilitators; avoid child-targeted pressure.

## Release rhythm
- Editorial pilot: complete one source-backed, reviewed book and use it to establish voice, evidence and production checks. Other collections can recover sources and outline in parallel without unbounded draft proliferation.
- First wave: assemble three strong complete works/editions per collection where the structure supports it; add a fourth only with its own purpose. Releasing one ready collection before another is allowed; do not market unfinished collections as ready.
- Quarterly review: examine completion/feedback, choose the next one or two useful works in each collection only where capacity supports them, and publish when ready. Quarterly is a review cadence, not a scheduled promise or automation.
- Long-term: finish the full series while preserving coherent reading paths. A new quarter can deliver a revised edition, workbook or audio adaptation only if clearly identified as such.

Before naming any calendar date, estimate from actual pilot throughput, founder interview availability, evidence review, age-specific editing, proofing and delivery tests. Aspirational totals are not a schedule.

## Bundles and reader journey
Start with finished single books and one focused bundle per ready collection. Cross-collection bundles follow once their components solve a coherent reader problem and are all ready. Do not offer a “complete series” while volumes are missing.

Each bundle record must list collection/book/edition IDs, exact manuscript versions, formats, cover alternatives, worksheet IDs, license/inclusions and exclusions, approval state and actual access/delivery evidence. Alternative covers are not extra books; duplicate works across bundles must be obvious.

Sell the contents available at purchase. Later quarterly volumes are separate unless a specific future entitlement has been deliberately approved and operationally supported. No default lifetime updates, automatic subscription, future-volume access or perpetual coaching. All bundle prices remain proposals. The $249 Academy journey and its human support remain separate.

Proposed journey: useful public lesson → optional sample and Major Move → clear single-book/complete-bundle comparison → fulfillment → optional feedback/community invitation. No app or wallet requirement to read a purchased book. Consent applies separately to marketing and community participation.

## Working metrics
Track editorial throughput and revision load; useful reader feedback, sample-to-product clicks, paid conversion with denominator/time window, delivery/access failures, refunds and opt-in repeat purchases once those events actually exist. Do not infer book completion from an opened download or promise revenue uplift from the prompt library. Use learning and trust alongside sales when deciding the next quarter's slate.

## Validation and boundaries
Documentation-only plan. Upstream version/license and relevant files inspected; internal source recovery addendum reused. Runtime tests/new telemetry are not applicable. GitHub commit/readback supplies provenance. Actual releases still require their editorial, rights, artifact and fulfillment evidence.
