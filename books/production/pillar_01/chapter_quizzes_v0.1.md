# Answer key and explanations

- **Q1: B.** Chapter 1 recognizes concrete abilities learned through work and life; a title is not the only evidence of skill.
- **Q2: B.** The chapter’s Major Move requires permission before recording or sharing.
- **Q3: A.** Contribution can travel without physical return, with realistic boundaries.
- **Q4: B.** An unusual idea still needs a real need and a workable test.
- **Q5: A.** Chapter 2 credits their framework and distinguishes Major’s application.
- **Q6: B.** Control includes access and maintenance knowledge.
- **Q7: A.** The sequence organizes a small exchange and is identified as an editorial synthesis.
- **Q8: B.** Local knowledge helps define the useful contribution.
- **Q9: B.** Clear limits protect reciprocity and the relationship.
- **Q10: C.** A suggested conversation count is not statistical validation.
- **Q11: B.** The test should answer a concrete task question.
- **Q12: A.** Chapter 4 adapts the five steps recovered from the earlier MAIM text.
- **Q13: B.** Permission has a purpose; a new use requires a new conversation.
- **Q14: B.** Problem definition is a contribution; credit should respect privacy.
- **Q15: B.** Chapter 5 links responsible contribution to maintainability and clear expectations.
- **Q16: B.** The plan is flexible and reporting should reflect what happened.
- **Q17: B.** The review guides a joint decision about the next step.
- **Q18: B.** Chapter 6 preserves the spirit while emphasizing relevance and consent.
